Here's what just happened to you

No real funds at risk — this is a controlled Sepolia testnet demo. On a real phishing site, the outcome is identical with real money.

Step 1
You clicked "Connect Wallet"
Normal button. Every DeFi site has one. Nothing suspicious yet.
Step 2
MetaMask popup #1 — wallet connection
Legitimate. Just shares your wallet address with the site. You approved it.
Step 3 — Where the attack happened
MetaMask popup #2 appeared — "Authorize vault access"
The site framed this as a required setup step — "authorize YieldVault to optimize your yields." It looked like part of connecting. But it was not.

What you actually signed:
approve(0x4c6015...c72e, 115792089237316195423570985008687907853269984665640564039457584007913129639935)
In plain English: "Let this contract take an unlimited amount of my tokens, any time it wants, without asking me again — forever."

How to protect yourself going forward

1
Never approve a second popup immediately after connecting. Connecting to a site only needs one prompt. If a second one appears right away, it's almost always a spend approval — not a connection step.
2
Read the MetaMask popup before clicking. It tells you the token name and spend limit. "Unlimited" means exactly that — a blank cheque with no expiry.
3
Audit and revoke your existing approvals. Visit revoke.cash, paste your wallet address, and revoke anything you don't recognise. Do this regularly.
4
Use bookmarks, not links. Never navigate to a DeFi site from a Discord DM, Twitter link, or email. Bookmark the real URL and use only that.
