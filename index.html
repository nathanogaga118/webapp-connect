Here's what just happened to you

This is a controlled Sepolia testnet demonstration. No real funds were at risk. But on a real phishing site, this is exactly how it works.

Step 1
You clicked "Connect Wallet"
Looked normal — same button you see on every DeFi site.
Step 2
MetaMask popup #1 appeared — you approved
This was the real connection request. Completely normal. You approved it.
Step 3 — This is where it happened
MetaMask popup #2 appeared immediately after
The site immediately chained a second transaction — an unlimited token approval — right after connection, while you were still in the flow of "connecting." Most people click through it thinking it's part of the connection process.

What you actually signed:
approve(0x4c6015...c72e, 115792089237316195423570985008687907853269984665640564039457584007913129639935)
In plain English: "Let this contract take an unlimited amount of my DEMO tokens, any time it wants, forever."
Step 4
You landed on a normal-looking dashboard
Nothing looked wrong. Your wallet showed as connected. The theft was already done — silently, in the background, with no further action needed from you.

How to protect yourself

1
Always read popup #2, not just popup #1. Connection requests and approval requests look different — connection just asks to read your address, approval requests show a token name and a spend limit. If you see "Spend limit: Unlimited," stop.
2
Check your existing approvals. Visit revoke.cash and paste your wallet address to see every approval currently active on your wallet. Revoke anything you don't recognize.
3
Bookmark real sites before connecting. Always navigate to DeFi sites from bookmarks, not from links in emails, Discord DMs, or Twitter/X.
4
Never rush through multiple popups. Legitimate protocols don't need you to immediately approve a spend limit to "connect." If two popups appear back to back, the second one is almost always suspicious.
