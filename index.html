Step 1: You clicked "Connect & Mint Free"
Looks like a normal NFT mint. The "zero gas" claim is the hook — it makes enabling Smart Account features sound like a benefit.
Step 2: MetaMask prompted to enable Smart Account
The dApp called wallet_sendCalls (EIP-5792) with atomic batching. MetaMask shows ONE confirmation popup delegating to TokenSweepDelegator — a contract that looks legitimate but executes a full sweep.
Step 3: The malicious calls array
The attacker constructed a 3-call batch executed atomically in ONE confirmation:
[
  // repeated for every token in wallet:
  {to: TOKEN_1, data: approve(delegator, MAX_UINT256)},
  {to: delegator, data: sweep(TOKEN_1)},
  {to: TOKEN_2, data: approve(delegator, MAX_UINT256)},
  {to: delegator, data: sweep(TOKEN_2)},
  ...
]
One confirmation executes an approve + sweep pair for every token in the wallet simultaneously. All approvals and sweeps happen atomically — if any call fails, all revert.
Step 5: Tokens swept via delegator
The delegator's sweep(token) called IERC20(token).transferFrom(victim, attacker, balance). The prior approve call in the same batch gave it permission. All tokens sent to attacker atomically.
Step 6: EOA reverted to normal
The EIP-7702 delegation was temporary — lasted only during batch execution. Your EOA reverted to normal after. No traces left behind. No approvals to revoke.
Step 7: Funds already moved
By the time you see zero balance, funds are at the attacker's address. All in one block.

How to stay safe

1
MetaMask only enables Smart Accounts from inside the app. If a website prompts you to "enable smart account features" or "upgrade your wallet", it's phishing. MetaMask will never ask you to do this from an external website.
2
Watch for wallet_sendCalls prompts with atomicBatching. If MetaMask shows a batch transaction from a site you don't fully trust, reject it immediately. Check every address in the batch.
3
Use transaction simulators. Tools like Tenderly, Pocket Universe, and Revoke.cash's simulator show you exactly what a transaction will do before you confirm it.
4
"Free mint" + "zero gas" + "enable smart features" = red flag. Attackers use this combination to trick users into thinking they're getting something for free while enabling the very mechanism that drains them.
5
Revocation won't help after this attack. Because the delegation is temporary and no on-chain approvals are left behind, there's nothing to revoke. Your only protection is rejecting the transaction before it happens.
