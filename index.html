Here's what just happened to you

No real funds at risk — this is a controlled Sepolia testnet demo. On a real phishing site, this is exactly how it plays out with real money.

Step 1
You clicked "Connect Wallet"
Normal button. Every DeFi site has one.
Step 2
MetaMask popup #1 — wallet connection
Legitimate. Just shares your public wallet address. You approved it.
Step 3 — Where it happened
MetaMask popup #2 — "Verify your wallet"
This looked like a harmless verification signature — same popup MetaMask shows when you log into OpenSea or any other site. No "Spending cap" warning. No mention of tokens. No transaction fee. Just "Sign."

But what you actually signed was a EIP-2612 Permit — a cryptographic authorization that lets the drainer contract pull your entire token balance, with no further action from you, no on-chain approval transaction ever recorded, and nothing for revoke.cash to find afterward.
Permit {
  owner: your wallet,
  spender: 0xdb61FA011dC61Afa7AB9d31604B281bb6bcfaf05,
  value: 115792089237316195423570985008687907853269984665640564039457584007913129639935,
  deadline: —
}
Why this is worse than approve() Unlike a standard approval, a permit signature leaves no on-chain record until it's used. Your wallet's approval list still shows clean. revoke.cash finds nothing. But the attacker holds your signed permit and can drain you at any moment — or sell the signature to someone else.

How to protect yourself going forward

1
Treat "Sign" popups as seriously as "Approve" popups. A signature request that mentions "Permit", "spender", "value", or "deadline" in its data is a permit — not a login. Read the typed data MetaMask shows before signing.
2
Never sign a second popup right after connecting. Connecting to a site only needs one approval. An immediate second request — transaction or signature — is almost always an attack.
3
Use a wallet that shows permit details clearly. Rabby Wallet decodes permit signatures and shows you the spender, value, and expiry in plain language before you sign. MetaMask's default view is less obvious.
4
Bookmark real sites. Navigate from bookmarks only — never from links in Discord, Telegram, Twitter/X, or emails. The URL is the only thing that separates this fake page from the real one.
